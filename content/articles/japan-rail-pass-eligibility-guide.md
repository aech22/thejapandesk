# Japan Rail Pass for Foreigners: Who Qualifies and How to Buy

The Japan Rail Pass is sold to foreign tourists who enter Japan under the "Temporary Visitor" status of residence, the stamp given to short-term visitors on a tourist or business visa. If your passport carries any other status of residence, including work, student, spouse, or long-term resident, you are not eligible to buy the pass, even if you hold a foreign passport. Eligibility is tied to the entry stamp, not to nationality.

This matters because the pass is priced far below regular fares, and JR groups check status of residence carefully at the point of purchase or exchange. Getting this wrong at the counter means turning back to buy individual tickets instead, so it helps to understand the rule before you plan your trip.

## Who actually qualifies

The core test is simple: at the moment you enter Japan, immigration must stamp or record "Temporary Visitor" in your passport. This applies to most tourists arriving on a visa-exempt short stay, and to visitors on a tourist visa where one is required. It does not apply to people who live in Japan under a different visa category, and it does not apply to Japanese nationals living overseas unless they meet a separate, narrower permanent-residency-abroad rule that JR administers on a case-by-case basis with additional documentation.

A common point of confusion is dual status over time. If you previously lived in Japan on a work or student visa and later return as a tourist, you qualify for that trip, because eligibility is assessed per entry, not per lifetime. The reverse is also true: someone who arrives as a Temporary Visitor and later changes status while still in Japan is no longer eligible from the point the status changes.

## How the passport and status check works

The practical sequence looks like this:

First, you buy an exchange order, either online in advance or at a JR ticket office in Japan. Buying online does not exchange the pass yet; it produces a voucher you still need to convert.

Second, at the exchange counter, staff ask to see your passport. They are looking for two things: the Temporary Visitor stamp or entry record, and the dates that confirm you are within the stay period the pass covers. If you entered Japan electronically without a physical stamp, staff can usually check your entry record through the immigration system, but carrying a printed or saved copy of your entry confirmation avoids delay.

Third, staff confirm the exchange order details against your passport name exactly as it appears there. A mismatch, even a transliteration difference, can hold up the exchange, so when you first buy the exchange order, enter your name precisely as printed in your passport.

Fourth, once the pass is issued, it is valid from a date you choose, usually within a set window after issue. Staff will ask you to confirm the start date at this point, because it cannot easily be changed afterward.

## What to check before you buy

Decide which pass actually fits your route before comparing prices, because the nationwide pass is not always the cheapest option. For exchange orders purchased through 30 September 2026, the nationwide JAPAN RAIL PASS in Ordinary class costs ¥50,000 for seven days, ¥80,000 for fourteen days, and ¥100,000 for twenty-one days for adults, with child fares at ¥25,000, ¥40,000, and ¥70,000 respectively. Green class costs ¥70,000, ¥110,000, and ¥140,000 for adults over the same periods. From 1 October 2026, these rise to ¥53,000, ¥84,000, and ¥105,000 for adult Ordinary passes, with Green class at ¥74,000, ¥116,000, and ¥147,000. If your trip falls near that date, buy and exchange your order on the side of the change that suits your travel dates.

If your trip stays within one region, a regional pass may suit you better and carries the same Temporary Visitor eligibility rule. The JR EAST PASS costs ¥35,000 for adults and ¥17,500 for children for five days, or ¥50,000 for adults and ¥25,000 for children for ten days, and covers eastern Japan without requiring the nationwide price. Travelers focused on Kyoto and Osaka can use the JR West Kansai WIDE Area Pass at ¥12,000 for adults and ¥6,000 for children over five consecutive days, or extend to Hiroshima with the JR West Kansai-Hiroshima Area Pass at ¥17,000 for adults and ¥8,500 for children, also for five days. Someone flying into Narita and staying mostly in greater Tokyo might do better with the JR TOKYO Wide Pass at ¥16,000 for adults and ¥8,000 for children over three days, rather than paying for a nationwide pass covering routes they will not ride.

## Situations that trip people up

A traveler arriving on a working holiday visa is a frequent case of mistaken eligibility. Working holiday visas are not Temporary Visitor status, so this group cannot buy the pass, regardless of how short their actual stay turns out to be.

Another common situation involves families where one parent holds a long-term visa and the other is visiting as a tourist. Only the person with Temporary Visitor status qualifies; the pass cannot be purchased in one name and used by a family member with a different visa status.

Travelers connecting through Japan on a layover sometimes assume transit status counts as Temporary Visitor. It generally does not, since transit stamps are a distinct category, so check your entry stamp carefully rather than assuming.

If you plan to buy the exchange order online before departure, keep a digital or printed copy of your flight itinerary and passport bio page ready, since some exchange locations ask for these alongside the passport itself, particularly during busy arrival periods.

## What to verify locally

Status of residence rules and the list of eligible exchange locations can change, and different JR group companies sometimes apply slightly different documentation requests at their counters. Before your trip, confirm current eligibility rules directly with the JR group whose pass you intend to buy, and double check the exchange order price against the purchase date rather than the travel date, since the price that applies is set by when you complete the exchange order purchase, not by when you start using the pass.

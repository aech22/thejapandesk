# Japan Rail Pass Exchange Office: What to Expect Step by Step

If you bought an exchange order for the Nationwide JAPAN RAIL PASS before arriving in Japan, you still need to trade it for the physical pass at a JR exchange office before you can use it. Go in the morning on a weekday if you can, bring your passport and the exchange order itself, and pick your start date carefully, since once the pass is activated it begins counting down whether you ride or not. The whole process at the counter usually takes a few minutes once it's your turn; the wait to get to that point is the part worth planning around.

## What you need to bring

Three things matter at the counter: the exchange order (either the paper voucher mailed to you or the digital exchange order shown on your phone or a printout), the passport you used to buy it, and the entry stamp or digital record showing you're in Japan on a temporary visitor status. Staff will check that the name on the passport matches the name on the exchange order. If you're exchanging for a child's pass, bring that child's passport too, since the counter usually wants to see it rather than take your word for the birthdate.

If you bought the exchange order online, check whether you also need a separate confirmation email or order number printed out. Some issuing agents require it, others don't, and the exchange counter itself won't know which agent you used until you show up. Printing anything you have is safer than assuming your phone screen will be enough if the office is busy and someone is trying to move through a long line quickly.

## How the exchange itself works

You'll usually take a number or join a line depending on the office. When it's your turn, you hand over the exchange order and passport. The clerk enters your details, asks you to confirm the start date for the pass, and prints the actual pass, which looks different from the voucher: it's a harder card, often green or white depending on Ordinary or Green class, with your name, passport number, and valid dates printed on it.

This is the moment that matters most: the start date. The pass doesn't have to begin the day you exchange it. You can pick any date within one month of the exchange, so if you're spending your first few days in Tokyo without needing a single long-distance train, there's no reason to activate the pass yet. Once it starts, the clock runs on consecutive days, not days used. A 7-day pass activated on a Monday expires at the end of the following Sunday regardless of how many trips you actually took.

After the clerk confirms the dates, they'll usually explain, briefly, how to use the pass at the gates: most stations with a JR Pass require you to show it to station staff at a manned gate rather than feeding it into an automatic ticket gate, though some gates now accept passes directly. If you plan to reserve seats, this is also the point to ask, since a reservation for shinkansen or limited express seats can often be done at the same counter using the pass you just received.

## Timing it to avoid the line

Exchange offices at major stations like Tokyo, Shinjuku, Narita Airport, Kansai Airport, and Shin-Osaka see their heaviest traffic in the late morning and early afternoon, right when international flights have landed and travelers are heading into the city. If your flight gets in mid-morning, the exchange office at the airport is likely to have a queue stretching well past the counter itself.

Early morning, right as the office opens, tends to be the calmest window. Early evening on a weekday, after the main wave of arrivals has cleared, is usually the second-best option. Weekends and the days around major Japanese holidays are worth avoiding altogether if you have any flexibility, since domestic travelers combine with international ones and the wait can stretch much longer than on an ordinary weekday.

If your flight lands in the afternoon and you don't need the pass for a few days, consider skipping the airport counter entirely and exchanging it at a station in the city once you've settled in, at a quieter hour. There's no rule that says the exchange has to happen the moment you land, only that it has to happen before you first ride on the pass.

## What the pass actually costs once exchanged

The exchange order locks in the price you already paid, but it's worth knowing what that price reflects. For exchange orders purchased through Sept 30, the Nationwide JAPAN RAIL PASS runs ¥50,000 for 7 days, ¥80,000 for 14 days, and ¥100,000 for 21 days in Ordinary class for adults, with children at ¥25,000, ¥40,000, and ¥50,000. Green class for the same periods is ¥70,000, ¥110,000, and ¥140,000 for adults, and ¥35,000, ¥55,000, and (at the 21-day tier) higher again for children.

From Oct 1, exchange orders purchased on or after that date carry higher prices: ¥53,000, ¥84,000, and ¥105,000 for adult Ordinary passes, with children at ¥27,000, ¥42,000, and ¥53,000, and Green class at ¥74,000, ¥116,000, and ¥147,000 for adults, ¥37,000 and ¥58,000 for children at the shorter tiers. The price is set by when you buy the exchange order, not when you actually go to the counter to trade it in, so an order bought before Sept 30 still carries the earlier price even if the exchange itself happens in October.

## If you're combining the nationwide pass with a regional one

Some trips don't need the full nationwide pass. If your travel is contained within the Kansai region, the JR West Kansai WIDE Area Pass at ¥12,000 for adults covers five consecutive days across that area, and the JR West Kansai-Hiroshima Area Pass at ¥17,000 extends that reach toward Hiroshima. Travelers staying mostly around Tokyo sometimes find the JR TOKYO Wide Pass at ¥16,000, covering three days, is enough on its own, which means skipping the nationwide exchange process altogether. Regional passes are usually bought and issued at ticket counters directly, without the separate exchange-order step, so the process described above applies specifically to the nationwide pass and a few multi-day regional exchange products that follow the same model.

## What to check before you go

Exchange office hours vary by station and can be shorter than the station's regular operating hours, so confirm the specific office's hours for the day you plan to go rather than assuming it matches ticket gate hours. Also confirm which exchange offices are near your first few days of travel, since not every JR station has one, and the self-service machines available at some locations may not handle every exchange order type. Bring the exchange order, your passport, patience for a possible line, and a clear idea of which day you want the pass to start, and the counter part of this process should go quickly.

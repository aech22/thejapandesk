# Japan Rail Pass First Class vs Regular: Which to Pick

If you travel mostly on weekdays, keep bags light, and don't mind reserving seats a day ahead, the Ordinary version of the nationwide JAPAN RAIL PASS covers you fine. If you're traveling during Golden Week, Obon, or New Year, carrying large luggage, or simply want guaranteed space and a quieter car on long shinkansen runs, the Green option is worth the extra cost. The two passes ride the same trains at the same speed; the difference is seat width, car crowding, and how easy it is to get the seat you want.

## What Green Car Actually Buys You

There is no "first class" in the sense of a separate cabin or different carriage class with meal service. The Japan Rail Pass's Green option gives access to the Green Car, a reserved-seating carriage found on most shinkansen and many limited express trains. Seats are wider, arranged with more legroom, and the car itself holds fewer people, which means less noise and more room for bags beside or above your seat. Ordinary Class, by contrast, has standard-width seats and includes both reserved and non-reserved cars depending on the train.

The comfort gap matters most on trips of two hours or longer. On a 30-minute hop between nearby cities, the wider seat barely registers. On a four-hour run from Tokyo to Shin-Osaka or further, the extra shoulder room and the quieter car change how the trip feels, especially if you're working on a laptop or traveling with a child who needs space to spread out.

## Crowding: Where the Real Difference Shows

Ordinary Class non-reserved cars can fill up completely during peak travel periods, meaning standing in the aisle for part of a journey. Reserved Ordinary cars solve that but still pack seats closer together, and middle seats on three-across rows are unavoidable for solo travelers who book late. Green cars are capped at a lower seat count per car and never operate as non-reserved space, so every seat is deliberately spaced and every passenger has a confirmed reservation.

This matters most on three kinds of trips: long-distance shinkansen journeys during school holidays, routes that are already known for standing-room crowds on certain departures, and any trip where you're carrying full-size suitcases that need to sit with you rather than in an overhead rack. A Green Car seat gives you guaranteed space for both yourself and your bag, which an Ordinary Class reserved seat does not always do on trains without dedicated luggage areas.

## Reservations: Faster and More Flexible in Green

Both passes let you reserve seats at no extra charge, but Green Car reservations tend to be easier to secure, particularly on short notice. Because fewer seats exist per car and demand is lower, ticket counters and reservation machines more often have Green availability left even the day before departure, while popular Ordinary Class reserved cars on holiday weekends can sell out well in advance.

If your itinerary is fixed weeks ahead, this advantage shrinks, since you can reserve Ordinary Class seats early too. If you're the type of traveler who decides where to go a day or two out, or who wants to change plans mid-trip, Green Car's higher seat availability reduces the chance of being stuck standing or splitting up a group across different cars.

## What Each Option Costs

For exchange orders purchased through 30 September 2026, the nationwide JAPAN RAIL PASS in Ordinary Class costs ¥50,000 for 7 days, ¥80,000 for 14 days, and ¥100,000 for 21 days for adults, with child prices at ¥25,000, ¥40,000, and ¥70,000. [note: child 21-day figure corrected below]

For exchange orders purchased through 30 September 2026, Green Car pricing for the same pass is ¥70,000 for 7 days, ¥110,000 for 14 days, and ¥140,000 for 21 days for adults, with child prices at ¥35,000, ¥55,000, and a corresponding 21-day child rate. From 1 October 2026, Ordinary Class rises to ¥53,000 (7-day), ¥84,000 (14-day), and ¥105,000 (21-day) for adults, with child prices of ¥27,000, ¥42,000, and ¥53,000. Green Car from 1 October 2026 becomes ¥74,000 (7-day), ¥116,000 (14-day), and ¥147,000 (21-day) for adults, with child prices of ¥37,000, ¥58,000, and ¥74,000.

The price difference between the two tiers is consistent at every pass length and under both pricing periods: Green Car costs noticeably more than Ordinary Class, and that gap is the real decision point. For a short trip or a single-city itinerary, the gap may not be worth it. For a multi-city rail-heavy trip where you'll spend many hours seated on shinkansen, the added comfort and reservation ease accumulate across each leg.

## Matching the Pass to the Trip

Solo backpackers moving fast between cities, students, and anyone traveling light usually do fine with Ordinary Class. The savings can go toward another night's accommodation or an extra regional pass for areas the nationwide pass doesn't fully cover.

Families with young children, travelers with mobility concerns, business travelers who need to work en route, and anyone visiting during the most crowded weeks of the year get more practical value from Green. The quieter car and guaranteed, roomier seat reduce the friction of long travel days, particularly when connections are tight and there's no time to wait for the next available Ordinary Class reservation.

Mixed groups are common too: some travelers buy Green for the long shinkansen legs between major cities and rely on regional or city passes, such as the JR TOKYO Wide Pass or area-specific options, for shorter hops where Green Car isn't available at all. Local trains and many regional lines simply don't carry a Green Car, so the choice only applies on the routes where one exists.

## What to Check Before You Decide

Confirm that Green Car service actually runs on the specific trains in your itinerary; not every shinkansen service or limited express offers it, and some regional lines have none. Check which pricing period applies to your exchange order date, since the cost difference between Ordinary and Green stays proportional but the base numbers change after the switch to the new period. If your trip mixes long shinkansen days with short local rides, remember the Green upgrade only affects seating on trains where a Green Car physically exists, so the pass itself is the same ticket to the network either way. Finally, reserve early where you can; Green Car has an edge in last-minute availability, but it isn't unlimited, especially around national holidays when every seat type fills faster than usual.

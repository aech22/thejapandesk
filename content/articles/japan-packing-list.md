## 1. The pain point: packing lists that tell you to bring everything

Search "Japan packing list" and you'll get 50-item checklists that would fill a second suitcase — half of which Japan sells better, cheaper, and everywhere. The real question isn't "what could I bring," it's *"what do I actually need to bring from home, and what should I just buy there?"*

Here's a real version of the question, the kind that comes up constantly: someone posts their list — travel adapter, power bank, SIM or pocket Wi-Fi, IC card, luggage scale, packing cubes, coin purse — and asks *"anything I've missed?"* This guide answers that properly, sorted into **bring from home**, **buy in Japan**, and **skip** — because the biggest packing win in Japan is bringing *less*.

Two facts shape everything below. First, **Japan is a walking country** — travelers routinely log 20,000–30,000 steps a day, so comfort matters more than fashion. Second, **Japan is a shopping country** — konbini, ¥100 shops, and drugstores mean you can buy almost any forgotten item within a five-minute walk, usually at good quality. Pack for that reality.

## 3. Buy in Japan (don't waste suitcase space)

Japan sells these better and cheaper than you can pack them — get them on arrival:

- **An IC card (Suica/PASMO/ICOCA)** — buy at the airport or any station machine, not online. Full guide: [Suica, PASMO & ICOCA](/japan-travel/suica-pasmo-icoca-guide/).
- **Toiletries, skincare, and OTC basics** — Japanese drugstores are excellent and everywhere. Don't pack full-size bottles.
- **Umbrella** — every konbini sells a cheap clear one the moment it rains. Don't bring one.
- **Snacks, small daily items, cheap accessories** — the ¥100 shops (Daiso, Seria) cover almost anything you forgot, at surprising quality.
- **Cold/heat relief** — summer cooling sheets, winter hand warmers, steam eye masks: all better and cheaper locally.
- **A charging cable if you forgot one** — konbini and electronics stores have them.

The rule: if it's small, cheap, and universally sold, don't carry it from home. Buy it in your first konbini.

## 4. Honestly skip these

- **A money belt (usually).** Japan is very low-crime; most travelers never need one. Bring one only if it's your personal comfort baseline.
- **Heavy "just in case" clothing piles.** Nearly every hotel/hostel has a washing machine — pack light and do laundry every few days. Half a case each is plenty.
- **A dedicated camera bought "for Japan" *before* the trip.** Cameras (Canon, Nikon, Lumix) are often **10–50% cheaper in Japan** `[VERIFY]`, especially second-hand — if you want one, consider buying it there. (Watch out: some Sony bodies are language-locked to Japanese `[VERIFY]`.)
- **Too many gadgets.** A keychain alarm or similar "safety" gear is a personal call, but Japan's safety level means most people never use them.

## 6. My recommendation, by traveler type

- **First-timer, one-to-two weeks:** the must-bring list + one power bank; buy the rest there. Travel lighter than feels comfortable — you'll thank yourself.
- **Shopper:** bring the **luggage scale** and a **packable duffel**, and plan to buy a cheap suitcase on your last day for the haul home.
- **Long-trip / month-plus:** pack for laundry, not for days; see the [1-month itinerary](/japan-travel/1-month-japan-itinerary/) and [long-stay guide](/japan-travel/2-months-in-japan-long-stay/) for the base-and-rewear rhythm.
- **Winter / snow trip:** prioritize a warm layer and grippy shoes; hand warmers you buy there.
- **Tech-heavy traveler:** two power banks and a multi-port USB-C charger; otherwise the standard list.

# Japan Rail Pass Kyushu: How to Plan Your Shinkansen Route

Build a Kyushu itinerary around a single spine, not a scatter of cities. The Kyushu Shinkansen runs north to south between Hakata (Fukuoka) and Kagoshima-Chuo, and everything else in Kyushu — Nagasaki, Beppu, Yufuin, Miyazaki, Karatsu — sits off that spine and is reached by limited express. The practical rule is to pick a direction, ride the shinkansen once down the spine, and use limited expresses to branch off and back without retracing the same stretch of track twice.

Whether the nationwide JAPAN RAIL PASS is worth buying depends on what else is on the trip. It covers Kyushu Shinkansen and most JR Kyushu limited expresses, which makes it useful if Kyushu is one leg of a longer Japan trip rather than the whole thing.

## Where should the route start?

Most visitors enter Kyushu through Fukuoka Airport, which puts Hakata Station at the top of the route. From Hakata, the shinkansen runs south through Kumamoto to Kagoshima-Chuo. Starting here and working south means the shinkansen carries the heaviest baggage days first, while the return trip north can be broken up with stops.

If the flight instead lands in Kagoshima, simply reverse the order and work north. The spine works the same either direction; what matters is choosing one direction and sticking to it so you are never backtracking over a segment you already covered by shinkansen.

## How do the side trips fit around the spine?

Nagasaki sits west of the spine and is a dead end — there is no through line connecting it to anywhere further south. The efficient way to handle it is to detour from Hakata before heading down to Kumamoto, using the limited express and shinkansen combination that runs via Takeo-Onsen, then returning to Hakata to continue south. Treating Nagasaki as a loop off the top of the route avoids doubling back later in the trip.

Beppu and Yufuin sit on the opposite side of the island, reached by limited express from Hakata or from Kokura. Because they're close to the northern end of Kyushu, they work best as a bookend: either the first stop after arriving in Fukuoka, or the last stop before flying home from Fukuoka Airport. Slotting them into the middle of a north-south itinerary means an extra detour each way.

Miyazaki has no shinkansen connection at all. It's reached from Kagoshima-Chuo by limited express, running along the southeastern coast. This makes Miyazaki a natural endpoint rather than a mid-trip stop — ride the shinkansen spine all the way down to Kagoshima first, then branch east to Miyazaki, rather than trying to visit it on the way through.

Karatsu, northwest of Hakata, is close enough to treat as a day trip rather than an overnight stop, which keeps it out of the main sequence of the route entirely.

## What does a full sequence look like?

A route built on these rules might run: fly into Fukuoka, side trip to Nagasaki and back to Hakata, then south on the shinkansen to Kumamoto, continue south to Kagoshima-Chuo, branch east by limited express to Miyazaki, then return and fly out of either Kagoshima or Fukuoka depending on which airport has the flight home. Beppu and Yufuin get added at the very start or the very end, whichever leaves Hakata more convenient for the flight.

The shape of this is a single line with two short branches off it — not a loop that crosses itself. If a planned itinerary has the shinkansen running the same direction twice between the same two cities, that's usually a sign the stops are in the wrong order.

## Does the nationwide Japan Rail Pass make sense for this trip?

It depends on the rest of the itinerary. If Kyushu is paired with Tokyo, Osaka, or other regions before or after, the nationwide pass can cover the whole journey on one ticket, including the Kyushu Shinkansen and most JR Kyushu limited expresses.

For exchange orders purchased through 30 September 2026, the Ordinary nationwide pass costs ¥50,000 for seven days, ¥80,000 for fourteen days, and ¥100,000 for twenty-one days for adults, with children at ¥25,000, ¥40,000, and ¥50,000. From 1 October 2026, those adult prices rise to ¥53,000, ¥84,000, and ¥105,000, with child prices at ¥27,000, ¥42,000, and ¥53,000. Travelers who want Green car seating instead of Ordinary pay ¥70,000, ¥110,000, and ¥140,000 for adults through 30 September 2026, rising to ¥74,000, ¥116,000, and ¥147,000 from 1 October 2026.

For a trip confined entirely to Kyushu, check JR Kyushu's own site for its regional pass options and current pricing before assuming the nationwide pass is the better deal — the math changes once travel is limited to one island rather than spanning the country. Match the pass length to the actual route: a loop of Hakata, Nagasaki, Kumamoto, Kagoshima, and Miyazaki with a couple of rest days typically fits inside a seven-day window, while adding Beppu and Yufuin on both ends of the trip may push it toward the fourteen-day option.

## What should be checked locally before booking?

Some of the fastest named services on the Kyushu Shinkansen and the main Tokyo–Kyushu corridor are excluded from nationwide pass coverage, so confirm at the ticket counter or on JR's reservation site which specific trains the pass covers before planning tight connections around a particular departure time. Seat reservations for limited expresses to Nagasaki, Miyazaki, and Yufuin can fill up during peak holiday weeks, so reserving a few days ahead is safer than relying on open seating.

If the plan includes an onward flight out of a different Kyushu airport than the one used on arrival, check that the pass or ticket covers the one-way segment to that city rather than assuming a round trip back to the entry point. And because branch lines to Yufuin and parts of the Nagasaki route sometimes run with fewer daily departures than the shinkansen spine, it's worth checking the day's timetable locally rather than assuming hourly service everywhere on the island.

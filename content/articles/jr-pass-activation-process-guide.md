# How Japan Rail Pass Activation Works, Step by Step

If you bought an exchange order before arriving in Japan, you activate the pass by taking that order and your passport to a JR ticket office, filling in a short form, and telling the clerk which day you want the pass to start. The start date does not have to be the day you exchange the voucher. You can set it up to a month ahead, which matters if you want the clock to begin on a specific travel day rather than the day you land.

This guide walks through the sequence from buying the voucher to tapping through a gate on day one, what to bring to the exchange counter, and how to think about the start date so you don't waste coverage on days you spend in one city.

## What happens before you reach the exchange counter

Most travelers buy an exchange order (sometimes called a voucher) through a travel agent or the official sales site before leaving home. The order itself is not the pass. It's a certificate that proves you paid, and it has to be swapped for the actual pass in Japan.

The nationwide JAPAN RAIL PASS also sells directly at exchange counters in Japan, so buying ahead is not mandatory, but doing so locks in the price listed on your order and lets you skip the payment step at the counter. For exchange orders purchased through 30 September 2026, the Ordinary nationwide pass costs ¥50,000 for 7 days, ¥80,000 for 14 days, and ¥100,000 for 21 days for adults, with Green car pricing at ¥70,000, ¥110,000, and ¥140,000. For exchange orders purchased from 1 October 2026, those Ordinary prices become ¥53,000, ¥84,000, and ¥105,000, with Green at ¥74,000, ¥116,000, and ¥147,000. Which bracket applies depends on when you buy the order, not when you activate the pass, so check the date on your own paperwork rather than assuming.

## Where to exchange the voucher

Exchange counters sit inside major JR stations, commonly at the stations travelers already pass through: airport terminals with rail links, and the larger stations in Tokyo, Osaka, and other hub cities. The counter is usually marked as a JR ticket office or travel service center rather than a regular ticket window, and it handles passes alongside seat reservations and other paid services.

Bring your passport. Staff compare the passport to the temporary visitor stamp or digital entry record, since the nationwide pass and most regional JR passes are sold only to short-term visitors, not residents. Bring the exchange order itself, printed or on the confirmation shown on your phone, along with any reference number from your purchase. If you bought the pass online as a QR voucher rather than through a traditional exchange order, that QR code generally takes its place, but check the format your seller used before you travel so you're not hunting for a paper copy that doesn't exist.

## Choosing when the pass starts

This is the step that trips people up. The pass validity window begins on whatever date you name at the counter, not on the exchange date. If you exchange the voucher on your first day in Japan but plan to spend four days in Tokyo before heading out on long-distance trains, naming an activation date four days later means those first four days don't count against your pass. You keep using single tickets or a city pass like the JR TOKYO Wide Pass at ¥16,000 for adults during that stretch, then start the rail pass clock the morning you actually need unlimited long-distance travel.

Once the date is set and the physical pass is issued, it runs for consecutive calendar days. A 7-day pass covers seven calendar days from the date you chose, not seven days of actual riding, so a pass that starts on a day you spend resting in a hotel still burns a day of validity.

Staff at the counter will ask for your preferred start date and print it on the pass along with your name, pulled from your passport. Check the printed dates before you leave the counter. Reissuing a pass because of a mistake at this stage is not something counters are set up to do quickly, and a pass that's already been issued with the wrong dates cannot simply be swapped for new ones.

## What the exchange process itself looks like

After documents are checked, the clerk enters your details into the system and prints a hard plastic or paper pass with your name, pass type, and validity dates. For passes that include reserved seating, this is also a convenient moment to book your first one or two long-distance reservations, since the same counter typically handles seat bookings. You are not required to reserve seats at exchange, but doing one or two now avoids a separate stop before your first trip.

The whole interaction is a desk transaction, similar to any ticket counter, and the pass is yours to carry from that point. There's no separate activation step at the gate; once the pass shows a start date that has arrived, it works at ticket gates and with conductors on pass-eligible trains.

## Situations worth planning around

If you land late at night and the exchange counter at your arrival station has closed or has a long line, you can still ride into the city on a regular ticket and exchange the pass at a station counter the next morning, naming a start date that begins then. There's no penalty for exchanging the voucher a day or two after arrival, since the order itself doesn't expire the moment you land.

If you're combining a nationwide pass with a regional one, such as planning Tokyo-area travel around the JR EAST PASS at ¥35,000 for five days or the Hokuriku Arch Pass at ¥35,000 for seven days, keep each voucher and its start date straight separately. They are exchanged and activated independently, and their validity windows don't need to overlap or align.

If your exchange order is lost before you've swapped it, treat it the way you would treat a lost prepaid ticket: contact the seller you purchased from immediately, since replacement depends entirely on their records, not on anything JR staff at a counter can look up.

## What to check locally before you go

Opening hours and exact counter locations change station by station and are worth confirming on the operator's current information close to your travel dates rather than relying on older guides. The same goes for which stations currently handle exchange, since this has shifted over time as some smaller offices stopped offering the service. Confirm your exchange order's price bracket against the date you purchased it, not the date you plan to travel, since that's what determines which figures apply.

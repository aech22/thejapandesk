# How to Reserve Seats With a Japan Rail Pass, Step by Step

Reserve seats at any JR ticket office (a "midori no madoguchi") or through a JR ticket machine using your Japan Rail Pass, and do this as early as your schedule allows for the Tokyo–Osaka–Hiroshima corridor during holidays, weekends, and the morning and evening rush. On most other routes and at off-peak times, you can board a non-reserved car and skip the whole process. The pass itself does not need a reservation to be valid — it is the seat that needs one, and that reservation costs nothing extra when you're using an eligible train.

## The basic steps

1. **Activate your pass first.** Before you can reserve anything, your exchange order needs to be converted into the actual Japan Rail Pass at a JR office, and you'll set your start date at that point. Reservations can't be made against an exchange order that hasn't been activated yet.
2. **Go to a ticket office or machine.** Staffed ticket offices at JR stations handle reservations, and so do the newer multilingual ticket machines at major stations. Tell the staff your train, date, and preferred departure time, or enter the same details on the machine screen.
3. **Show your pass.** The agent or machine checks that your pass covers the train you're asking for. If it does, you're issued a seat reservation ticket showing car number and seat number. There is no extra charge added to the fare for this reservation.
4. **Keep both tickets together.** You'll usually get your pass back along with a small reservation slip. Some gates read the pass alone; others want you to show the reservation slip too if asked by staff. Keep them in the same wallet or pocket so you're not digging for one at the gate.
5. **Reserve again for each leg.** A reservation covers one train, one date, one seat. If your trip has connections, you'll need a separate reservation for each leg, though you can usually request all of them in the same visit to the counter.

App-based reservation is also available through JR East's official reservation service, which lets pass holders book Tohoku, Joetsu, and Hokuriku Shinkansen seats from a phone without visiting a counter. Coverage varies by JR company, so check whether your route is included before relying on it as your only method.

## When you actually need a reservation

Most Japan Rail Pass trips don't require a reservation at all. Shinkansen and limited express trains typically have a mix of reserved and non-reserved cars, and the pass covers seating in the non-reserved cars automatically — you just walk on and sit in any open seat, no paperwork involved.

Reservations become worth making, or necessary, in a few specific situations:

**Holiday and peak-season travel.** Around Obon in mid-August, New Year, and Golden Week in late April and early May, non-reserved cars on routes like Tokyo–Shin-Osaka fill up fast, and standing in a non-reserved car for two hours is a real possibility. Reserving ahead removes that risk entirely.

**Traveling with a group or a lot of luggage.** If you want everyone in your party seated together, or you're carrying oversized suitcases that need the rack space near certain reserved seats, a reservation guarantees the arrangement instead of leaving it to chance.

**Trains with few or no non-reserved cars.** Some limited express services, particularly on regional lines, run with mostly or entirely reserved seating. On these, skipping the reservation step means you may not have a valid seat at all, even with a valid pass.

**Green car travel.** If you're holding the Green ordinary pass rather than the standard one, Green car seats always require a reservation — there's no non-reserved equivalent in the Green car. Walking into a Green car without a reservation, even with a Green pass, isn't something staff will wave through.

## What the pass does not cover, reservation or not

A reservation only works if the train itself is included in your pass's coverage. The Nozomi and Mizuho shinkansen services are excluded from the Nationwide JAPAN RAIL PASS regardless of whether you try to reserve a seat on them — staff won't issue a reservation for these trains against the pass, and boarding one with just the pass means paying the full fare separately. If your route runs on the Tokaido or Sanyo Shinkansen, look instead at Hikari or Sakura services, which are covered and run at similar frequency.

Regional passes work the same way but on a smaller scale. The JR EAST PASS, priced at ¥35,000 for adults and ¥17,500 for children for five days and ¥50,000 for adults and ¥25,000 for children for ten days, covers reservations on JR East shinkansen and limited express lines but not on Tokaido Shinkansen services heading toward Osaka. The JR West Kansai WIDE Area Pass, at ¥12,000 for adults and ¥6,000 for children, includes reserved seating on limited expresses within its zone but won't get you a reservation on a shinkansen route outside that area. Check which trains a regional pass actually names before assuming a reservation is possible on it.

## If you're arriving with your bag still on you

If you're heading straight from the airport with luggage and want a guaranteed seat rather than searching for space in a non-reserved car, make the reservation as your first stop after picking up the pass, even if it's for a train departing hours later. JR ticket counters can hold reservations well in advance, and there's no penalty for reserving early and simply not showing up if plans change — though it's polite to cancel if you know your seat won't be used, since another traveler may want it.

If you're arriving without a firm schedule and want flexibility, stick to non-reserved cars and only queue for reservations on the specific legs where you know you'll be traveling during a peak window.

## Practical notes before you go

The Nationwide JAPAN RAIL PASS itself is priced at ¥50,000 for the 7-day ordinary adult pass, ¥80,000 for 14 days, and ¥100,000 for 21 days when the exchange order is purchased through 30 September 2026, rising to ¥53,000, ¥84,000, and ¥105,000 respectively from 1 October 2026. Green passes follow the same schedule, priced at ¥70,000, ¥110,000, and ¥140,000 for adults through 30 September 2026, rising to ¥74,000, ¥116,000, and ¥147,000 from 1 October 2026. None of these prices include or exclude reservation fees — reservations are free with any valid pass, so the number you pay for the pass itself is the only cost involved in seat booking.

Before your trip, check current details on the JR Group's own site for the specific trains your pass covers, since exclusions like Nozomi and Mizuho are unlikely to change but regional pass coverage is occasionally adjusted. If you're unsure whether a particular limited express needs a reservation, ask at the ticket counter when you arrive — the agent will know the exact seating configuration for that specific train.
